🎙 Whisper Benchmark

44 recordings (40 analysed)  ·  84.8 min audio  ·  backends: local + whisper-1, gpt-4o-transcribe

Summary

Three speech-to-text systems transcribed the same 44 voice recordings. 4 nearly empty ones were left out of the statistics, so 40 files (83 minutes) were compared on speed, cost and agreement with a reference.

  • Speed: gpt-4o-transcribe took 2.4 minutes in total, the local model 46 minutes, about 19 times longer. The local model ran on the processor only.
  • Cost: $0.51 for the whole corpus with either online model ($0.006 per minute); local costs nothing per file.
  • Agreement with the reference (gpt-4o-transcribe), error rate: local: mean 0.094, median 0.086; whisper-1: mean 0.099, median 0.046. Averages are close, the online whisper-1 is better at the median.
  • Where local is weaker: long recordings (3 minutes and more) and repetitions: words or whole phrases are repeated, 13 times in 7 files, sometimes several times in a row.
  • Caution: the reference is itself a machine transcription, so the error rates show agreement, not truth. Only 40 files from one speaker, one run on one server.
About this report — what was measured, how, and what the abbreviations mean

What was done

The same 44 voice recordings (85 minutes in total, natural speech, mixed German and English, recorded on a phone) were transcribed by three speech-to-text systems. 4 recordings with almost no speech were left out of the speed and error statistics; the remaining 40 (83 minutes) were compared. Speed, cost and agreement between the systems were measured. The goal: is it worth paying for an online service, or is a model that runs on the home server good and fast enough?

The three systems

  • local — whisper.cpp with the model large-v3-turbo (compressed to 5 bit, “q5_0”), running on the server’s processor (CPU), no graphics card. Language is detected automatically. Nothing leaves the machine.
  • whisper-1 — OpenAI’s online transcription service with the classic Whisper model.
  • gpt-4o-transcribe — OpenAI’s newer, GPT-4o based online transcription model. Both online services cost $0.006 per audio minute.

How it was measured

  • Time: stopwatch around the transcription call. Local: only the run of the program (including loading the model each time); the preceding conversion of the audio to a 16 kHz WAV file is not counted. Online: from sending the request (including upload of the audio file) until the text is back. Files were processed one after the other, once each.
  • Reference: there is no human-written transcript. gpt-4o-transcribe serves as the reference, so its own error rate is 0 by definition. All error rates therefore measure agreement with gpt-4o-transcribe, not absolute correctness. Both of the other systems can be right where the reference is wrong.
  • Settings: the online services received the original audio file with default settings (no language given, no hint text, plain-text output). Local used automatic language detection and no time stamps.

How the comparison was performed

  1. Pairing: for each recording, the text of a system is compared with the reference text of the same recording.
  2. Cleaning (applied to both texts before comparing): everything is made lowercase; hyphens, dashes and slashes become spaces; all other punctuation is deleted (also apostrophes, so “don’t” becomes “dont”); repeated spaces and line breaks are collapsed. Letters with umlauts and ß as well as digits are kept.
  3. Counting: the cleaned texts are split into words and aligned by the minimum number of changes (word-level edit distance, library jiwer). Each replaced, missing or extra word counts as one error, no matter how small the difference is. WER = errors / words in the reference. If a system returns no text for a recording whose reference has words, that counts as WER 1.0 (every word missing).
  4. Which files count: a recording whose reference has fewer than 10 words is left out of all speed and error statistics (4 files). One word is then a 10–100% error, which says nothing about quality. These files stay in the table (greyed out) and in the cost total.
  5. Per file, then average: the WER is calculated per recording, and mean, median and p95 are taken over the 40 analysed recordings. Every file counts equally, so a 2-minute recording weighs as much as a 7-minute one (the median is often more informative than the mean).

Ignored (does not count as an error): upper/lower case, punctuation, line breaks and paragraph layout, hyphen or slash versus space.

Not ignored (counts as an error): different spelling of the same word (for example “Handynummer” versus “Handy-Nummer” or “Handy Nummer” is an error, because one word becomes two; “Handy-Nummer” versus “Handy Nummer” is not), digits versus spelled-out numbers (“2” versus “zwei”), filler words (“äh”, “um”), contractions, names, wrong language, and every word the reference has and the other text does not (or the other way round).

Not taken into account at all: whether a mistake changes the meaning (a harmless slip counts the same as a serious one), whether the reference itself is correct, punctuation and formatting quality, speaker or time information, audio quality, memory use and electricity of the local model, and variation between repeated runs (every file was run only once per system). Apple’s own automatic transcripts of the recordings were considered at first and then dropped as unreliable.

Highlighting in the full report (not in this public version) uses its own simpler comparison in the browser (lowercase, plain letters only) and is only for display. It does not influence any number here.

Abbreviations and terms

RTF — real-time factor
Transcription time divided by audio duration. 0.05 means a 10-minute recording took 30 seconds. 1.0 is exactly real time, above 1.0 is slower than the recording itself. Lower is better.
WER — word error rate
(S + D + I) / N: the number of replaced (substitutions), missing (deletions) and extra (insertions) words, divided by the number of words N in the reference. 0 = identical, 0.10 = about one word in ten differs. Values above 1 are possible if there are more errors than reference words. Lower is better.
Mean / median / p95
Average; the middle value (half of the files are better, half worse; less sensitive to outliers); and the value that 95% of the files stay below, i.e. the bad end.
n
Number of files in a calculation.
Cost
Audio minutes × $0.006, in US dollars, for all files. Local costs nothing per file.
Time
Seconds for one file. Duration is the length of the recording.
Greyed-out row
A recording that was left out of the statistics because it has almost no speech.
Rec 01 …
Anonymous number of a recording (in chronological order).
CPU / GPU
Normal processor / graphics processor. Speech models run much faster on a GPU.
API
Interface to an online service.

Reading the charts and the table

  • Speed (scatter): every dot is a file. The higher a system’s dots sit, the longer it took for the same duration. Charts show the analysed files only.
  • RTF per file (bars): the same in relative terms. Bars above 1 mean slower than real time.
  • WER distribution: how many files fall into each error band. Many files on the left (0–10%) is good.
  • Table: click a column header to sort. The filter box searches the file name. Colours: green < 20%, yellow < 40%, red above. Greyed-out rows are the excluded recordings.

Observations

  1. Speed is the clear difference. For 83 minutes of audio, gpt-4o-transcribe needed 2.4 minutes in total, local 46 minutes: local was about 19 times slower (about 21 times by mean RTF). gpt-4o-transcribe is also slightly faster than whisper-1. Local was slower than real time (RTF above 1) on 6 of 40 files.
  2. Local speed depends on file length. Short files carry a fixed cost, because the model is loaded for every call (this is a likely explanation, not measured separately). Median local RTF falls from 0.93 for files under a minute to 0.48 for files of 3 minutes and more. The online services are fast at every length.
  3. Cost is a non-issue at this volume. Both online models charge $0.006 per minute: $0.51 for the whole corpus (all 44 files were billed). 30 hours of voice notes per month would be about $10.80. Local costs nothing per file, but occupied the server’s processor for 46 minutes for these files.
  4. Accuracy: close on average, but different by recording length. Against the reference, local and whisper-1 have similar mean error rates (see the cards above). By recording length the picture differs; in this run, local was clearly further from the reference on the longest recordings:

  5. The local model sometimes repeats words or whole phrases. A word or phrase directly followed by itself occurs 13 times in the local transcripts, spread over 7 files (whisper-1: 3 in 2 files; gpt-4o-transcribe: 0 in 0 files). 11 of the local cases are phrases of two or more words, and 12 repeat a unit that ends a clause or sentence, i.e. the end of a sentence is said again, in some cases three or four times in a row before the text moves on. 6 of the 7 affected files are longer than 3 minutes. That looks like the known “repetition loop” of Whisper-type models. Some repeats are real speech (stuttering, a list of names, “like like”), so not every case is an error. Every wrongly repeated passage adds insertions and thus raises the WER of the local model; the reference text has no such passages.
  6. 4 nearly empty recordings were left out. Their reference has fewer than 10 words (very little speech). One word more or less is then a 10–100% error, which says nothing about quality, and a very short clip can also distort the speed figures. They are excluded from all speed and error statistics, stay in the table (greyed out) and in the cost total. Mean WER with and without them:
